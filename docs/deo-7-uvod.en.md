# Part VII — The program matures

## Before we start: the end of the book, not the end of the program

The last part of the book doesn't introduce a new observation technique
— every mechanism, from Chapter 1 through Chapter 29, has already been
presented. What these three chapters do is show what all of it looks
like together, applied to a real, time-stretched program rather than an
isolated example:

- **Chapter 30** follows what the phased rollout actually looked like in
  real time — including deviations from the plan, and why some of those
  deviations weren't a sign of weakness, but a sign that the team was
  correctly reacting to what it discovered along the way.
- **Chapter 31** asks a different question: how a team measures the
  maturity of its own program, not once, but as a repeatable, periodic
  discipline — because one good check doesn't guarantee the next one
  will pass the same way.
- **Chapter 32**, the conclusion, introduces nothing new — it gathers
  the rules from every previous part of the book in one place and
  returns to the image the book opened with: an aircraft carrying two
  systems for recording its own state, one for questions asked in
  advance, the other for what no one could have predicted.

The point of this part — and of the whole book — isn't that there's a
moment when an observability program is "done." Chapters 30 and 31 show
what "not done, but under control" looks like in practice, week by week.
Chapter 32 closes the loop. What remains after that is no longer up to
the book — it's up to the reader, and the system they're building.
